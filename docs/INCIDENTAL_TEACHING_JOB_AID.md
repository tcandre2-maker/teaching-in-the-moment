# Job Aid: Teaching in the Moment
## A quick reference for RBTs

**Core idea:** Notice naturally occurring opportunities, connect them to an authorized goal, and follow the client's written treatment plan. Do not create a new target or change the procedure independently.

## Six-step implementation sequence

| Step | What to do | Check before proceeding |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Observe | Notice what the learner is doing, attending to, approaching, communicating, or moving away from. | Describe the behavior in observable terms. |
| 2. Recognize an authorized opportunity | Determine whether the moment connects to a goal already in the treatment plan. | Is this goal authorized in this activity/setting? |
| 3. Allow the planned response opportunity | Follow the response opportunity and timing specified in the plan. | Is the communication response available and accessible? |
| 4. Prompt only if needed | Use only the prompt and sequence written in the plan. | Does the plan specify the next prompt for this context? |
| 5. Reinforce as written | Deliver the programmed consequence or assistance as specified. | Does the consequence match the plan and learner's response? |
| 6. Record | Document the response, prompt level, and other required information as directed. | Am I recording what occurred rather than inferring intent? |

## Before acting: four quick questions

1. **What did I observe?** Identify the learner's observable action or communication.
2. **Which written goal applies?** Locate the actual target and response definition.
3. **What is the next authorized step?** Check the setting, response form, timing, prompting, reinforcement, and data directions.
4. **Is anything unclear or outside my role?** If a meaningful procedure is missing or unclear, pause and consult the supervising BCBA.

## Stop and consult when

- The relevant target is not in the treatment plan.
- The setting or response form is not covered by the plan.
- The next prompt or prompt sequence is not specified.
- A proposed action would change reinforcement, response definitions, mastery criteria, or another treatment procedure.
- You are unsure whether the learner's response meets the written definition.
- The learner withdraws, signals stop, or shows a response that requires following the plan's safety/assent procedures.

Follow the client's plan and organizational safety policies. Do not continue an uncertain scored procedure while waiting for clarification.

## Avoid these nonexamples

- Hiding or removing communication materials to force a different response.
- Moving preferred items farther away solely to manufacture a teaching trial.
- Requiring speech when AAC, sign, gesture, or another form is authorized.
- Interrupting a correct independent response to prompt unnecessarily.
- Inventing a new target because an activity looks like a learning opportunity.
- Selecting a familiar prompt hierarchy from memory when the plan is unclear.

## Documentation reminder

Record the observable response and the level of independence/prompting as required by the treatment plan and data system. Do not infer motivation, intent, or mastery from a single moment unless the written criteria support that conclusion.

**Keep the boundary clear:** Recognize and implement authorized opportunities; do not independently design or modify treatment.
